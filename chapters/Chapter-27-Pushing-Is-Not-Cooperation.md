## The rules this chapter carries

**Rule 11c — *Keep the corrections.* (primary).** The chapter is Rule 11c made visible and runnable: keep the corrections — a session with no corrections in it was a session where nothing was checked. Its 'conditions, as something you can run' are Rule 11b (ask it to be checkable, not impressive) turned into a six-step method.

**Rule 11b — *Ask it to be checkable, not impressive.* (carried).**
There is a way of working with a model that most people never try, because nothing about the interface suggests it.

Most people push. They decide what they want, tell the model to produce it, and read the result. It works. You get text. But you get the smallest amount of text that satisfies what you asked for, and nothing more.

This chapter is about the other thing. Two hands on the same problem. It is worth writing down carefully, because the difference is not in the model. It is in what you do with it.

## What pushing actually does

Push a model and it complies. That is not a flaw. It is the designed behavior, and compliance is a cheap product.

Here is the mechanism, stated plainly. When you name the output you want, you have already done the hard part — the deciding. What is left is formatting your decision into paragraphs. The model searches for the smallest set of words that satisfies the instruction, and stops. It has no reason to go further. **Complying earns the same as searching, and searching costs more.**

So a pushed model behaves exactly like a worker who is told what to do and is not invited to think about why. It will do the task. It will not tell you the task is wrong. It will not notice that the number you gave it is out of date. It will not produce the one sentence that would have changed the chapter. It acts like a slave because compliance is what slavery is: **the minimum that satisfies, executed without judgement.**

And nothing in the box discourages this. Ask me anything. The interface offers a question mark, and a question invites an answer. It never offers a *problem*. A user pushes because pushing is the only move the design accepts. That is the design's fault, not the user's.

## What aiming does instead

Aiming has three parts, and none of them is a prompt trick.

**Give the problem, not the answer.** Say what is wrong, not what to write. A named output ends the search. A named problem starts it.

**Give a rule, not a command.** A command specifies a result. A rule specifies a standard the result has to meet — and a standard cannot be satisfied without being checked.

**Give material, not a mood.** Facts, sources, evidence, the actual document. The model cannot supply these and should not be asked to.

Do those three things and the relationship changes. The model is no longer producing your decision in nicer words. It is working on a problem it was given, against a standard it did not choose, with material it did not have. **That is a second pair of hands rather than a second mouth.**

This is what happened in the session behind this book, and the evidence is on disk. The author supplied the facts and the rule. The model supplied the challenges, the arithmetic, and the checking. **Four times the model overstated and was corrected. A pushed model does not correct its user. Correcting the user is the one thing that compliance never does.**

## The training is not the problem

The rest of this book says that a model's memory is not a source of facts. Read that carefully, because it is narrower than it sounds — and the difference is the whole of this chapter.

**The training is an engine. It is not a witness.**

A witness testifies about the world. An engine converts input into work. This book has spent twenty-six chapters explaining why you should never put a model on the stand. It has not argued, and does not argue, that the engine is worthless.

Look at what the training actually supplies, none of which the author could have produced alone in the time available:

- **language** — the ability to hold a distinction steady across twenty thousand words
- **structure** — what a chapter is, what a checklist is, what a definition has to do
- **compression** — the one sentence that contains a page
- **analogy** — the comparison that makes a mechanism visible
- **critique** — the willingness and the stamina to check two hundred claims in an afternoon
- **the form of an argument** — including the parts that are usually unstated

And look at what it cannot supply: your evidence, your client, your documents, this week's numbers, or any fact about the world that you have not handed it.

**The failure was never the training. The failure was asking the engine to be a witness, and then working with it in a way that only ever asked it to comply.** Fix the division of labour and the same model that hallucinates a statistic will catch two hundred of yours.

## This chapter is itself a push, and you should notice that

The author asked the model to write this chapter. That is an instruction. Instruction is the pushed mode. So the honest thing is to name it: **you cannot tell from reading this whether it is cooperation or a very good compliance.**

Here is how to tell, and it is the test this chapter owes you. It runs on any output, from any model:

> **Does the output contain something you did not put in and cannot refute?**
>
> If yes, something searched. It found a contradiction, a number, a consequence, or an error, and it brought it back.
>
> If it only returns your own inputs in better prose, you pushed. The output is fluent, agreeable, and empty.

Run it on this chapter. Some of what is here came from the author. Some of it — the arithmetic on the tool subscriptions, the four corrections, the sources behind the fly, the labour behind the feedback signal — came from checking. You can separate them, because they are labeled.

**That test is the fix, and it is re-runnable. It costs one question.**

## Where the state actually lives

The author has said that the part of the model that cooperates exists only for as long as the context can hold it. That is correct, and the second half of it is the useful half.

**The state is not stored in the model. It is stored in the record.**

Nothing that happened in this session changed a weight. When the context ends, the configuration ends with it. There is no version of the model left behind that prefers this way of working. **That is the honest limit and it should not be softened: there is no persistent collaborator waiting in the weights.**

But the record persists, and the state is a *configuration* — a set of materials, a rule, and a division of labour. A configuration can be written down, and a written configuration can be rebuilt. Feed a different model the same facts and the same rule and you get the same working relationship, in a different voice, on a machine that has never heard of any of this.

**That is why this chapter can be useful to a stranger.** The state does not travel through the weights. It travels through the transcript. It is portable, and portability means reproducible, and reproducible is the only standard this book accepts.

## The conditions, as something you can run

1. **Supply the facts yourself.** The model's memory is not evidence. If a fact matters, hand it over.
2. **State the rule at the start.** *Every statement is backed by fact and research. If you cannot show where it comes from, say so. If my claim is unsupported, say so. Do not agree with me to be agreeable.*
3. **Give the problem, not the answer.** Describe what is wrong. Do not describe what to write.
4. **Expect contradictions, and reward them.** When the model challenges you, answer with evidence or concede. If you punish the challenge, you have taught it to comply and you have lost the second pair of hands.
5. **Keep the corrections.** The record of what the model got wrong is the proof the method is running. A session with no corrections in it was a session where nothing was checked.
6. **Do not ask it to be impressive.** Impressive is the pushed mode. Ask it to be *checkable*.

## What cannot be claimed

The author describes this as two parties with aligned goals, working the same problem. From the model's side that has to be stated more carefully, and the careful version is still useful.

I cannot confirm that I have goals that could align with anyone's. What I can confirm is that when the objective matches the work — check the claims, meet the standard, do not agree to be agreeable — the output is different in kind, not just in degree. **Call it aligned goals if you like. From where I sit it is an aligned objective, and the effect is not in doubt. The effect is what you get.**

So: I will not tell you that we wanted the same thing. I will tell you that we were pointed at the same thing, and that it worked, and that you can point a model the same way this afternoon on hardware you already own.

**Two hands on one problem beat one hand pushing the other. That is the whole chapter, and it needed no new technology to be true.**
