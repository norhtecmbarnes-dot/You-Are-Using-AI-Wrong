## From the plan (00-BOOK.md section 7 — added 14 October 2026)

- Claim: the promise economy sells **outcomes** — make a million dollars with AI, go viral, replace
  the workforce — and **a wish has no ingredients; work does.** A genie grants; a tool performs work
  on what you supply.
- Demo: the sourced receipts — a startup spending more on tokens than on headcount; a lender that
  recommitted to human support because cost-maximization produced "lower quality"; a retailer that
  retrained rather than replaced; and the 2023 salary headline the promise-sellers inflated past its
  documentation.
- Fix: bring the work. **AI is not a genie — it does not grant wishes.**

## Evidence already verified for this chapter

*(from `00-BOOK.md` section 9 — verified 14 October 2026. Quoted as it stands, including its
⚠️ cautions; those are part of the material and must travel with it.)*

- **Tokens exceeding headcount — Mercor (June 2026).** CEO Brendan Foody on the *20VC* podcast
  (reported by Business Insider): *"Right now we're spending more on tokens for our internal agents
  than we are on employee head count... That's correct. It's pretty incredible."* Mercor had roughly
  300 employees (PitchBook, October 2025). **Grade: a chief executive's on-the-record statement, not
  audited accounts** — attribute as his claim.
- **Compute beyond payroll — Nvidia (2026).** Bryan Catanzaro, VP of applied deep learning, told
  Axios the team's compute cost has already surpassed the cost of employing people. Same grade.
  Corroborating color, not load-bearing: Uber's 2026 AI-coding budget reportedly spent by April; a
  25-person company reported with AI costs above its entire payroll.
- **The rehired humans — Klarna (May 2025).** After shrinking its workforce roughly 22% through
  attrition and leaning on an AI assistant it had credited with the work of 700 agents, Klarna
  publicly recommitted to human customer support: *"As cost unfortunately seems to have been a too
  predominant evaluation factor... what you end up having is lower quality"* (CEO Sebastian
  Siemiatkowski, to Bloomberg). ⚑ **Carry the company's own counter-framing:** Klarna says it is
  *not* a reversal — "an addition, not a rehire" — and the assistant then did the work of **over 800
  full-time roles** (the company's correction of the widely quoted "700"). The precise story is
  sharper than the meme: **a workforce deliberately thinned, then human quality bought back —
  because the maximization goal itself produced the failure.**
- **Principle before prompts — IKEA (Reuters 13 Jun 2023; Fortune 30 Jul 2026).** Since 2021, Ingka
  Group retrained roughly **8,500 call-centre workers** as interior-design advisers and complex-query
  specialists as the Billie assistant absorbed routine queries — 47% of customer queries across its
  first two years, ~€13M operating savings (Ingka's June 2023 report), a 74% assist rate by 2026;
  Fortune's words: *"remaking — not replacing."* ⚑ The dictated claim ("retrained all of those
  employees to be designers") **survives at the narrower scope**, and at that scope it is checkable
  end to end. (At drafting, decide which chapter this receipt serves — 28 uses it as proof a wish can
  be met with work; 29 uses it as proof the work followed a principle.)
- **The salary promise (verified 14 Oct 2026).** The documented 2023 headline: Anthropic's
  *"Prompt Engineer and Librarian"* listing at **$175K–$335K** base (The Verge's report of the
  listing; eFinancialCareers, January 2023: *"The $335k engineering job where you don't write any
  code"*); later compilations cite a **$250K–$375K** band from Bloomberg/Time/Washington Post
  coverage. ⚠️ **The ranges disagree — quote one and name it.** The promise economy's version climbed
  past the documentation (*"$500K a year"*): the documented claim was already enormous, and the hype
  inflated it.
- **The wealth superlative (verified 14 Oct 2026).** Forbes: Elon Musk has been the world's richest
  person since **May 2024**; $936B on 1 Oct 2026; regained trillionaire status the first week of
  October 2026. ⚠️ **The fastest-dating figure in the book** — re-verify at print or drop the clause;
  the argument does not need it.
