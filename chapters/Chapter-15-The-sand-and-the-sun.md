## Evidence

*(from `00-BOOK.md` section 9 — the book's evidence register. Quoted as it stands, including its own ⚠️
cautions. The cautions are part of the material and must travel with it.)*

- **The cognitive-offloading finding** (Ch. 9, Rule 10) — *students performed better with the tool and worse
  without it; the capability didn't build.* Plus: *generative AI boosted learning for those who engaged in deep
  conversation and explanation, and hampered those who sought direct answers.* **This is the evidence under this
  chapter's "why bother."**

**⚠️ The three citations behind that finding — and one hard caution:**

| Ref | Citation | Note |
|---|---|---|
| [19] | Bastani H, et al. **"Generative AI Can Harm Learning."** 2024. SSRN 4895486 | Working paper, **not peer-reviewed** — say so. |
| [20] | Darvishi A, et al. **"Impact of AI Assistance on Student Agency."** *Computers & Education.* 2024;210:104967 | **Peer-reviewed. Lean on this one hardest.** |
| [21] | Lehmann M, Cornelius PB, Sting FJ. **"AI Meets the Classroom: When Does ChatGPT Harm Learning?"** 2024. SSRN 4941259 | Working paper. Its title is literally the book's question. |

**⚠️ `PMC12255134` is a protocol and reports no results — never cite it as findings.** It is the design document
for a randomized controlled trial (*Trials*, 2025; NCT06511102) proposing to measure cognitive effort with
pupillometry and fNIRS imaging. It cannot support *"it has been shown."* **The chapter's factual claim is the
finding above, not "brains develop less," which the research has not shown.**

**⚠️ And this is the chapter where the least should be claimed.** Its material is the argument and the reader's
own senses, not a source to cite. The emotional peak is not a place to put a statistic.

## The rules this chapter carries

**Rule 3 — *There is no one in there.*** The primary rule. The chapter is the embodiment half of Concept 3: the
model can produce *"I feel relaxed,"* and there is no one there to feel it. **Argue experience, never *"machines
can't think"*** — the second is a claim about capability and it is attackable; the first is a claim about
experience and it is not.

**Rule 10 — *Delegating the thinking costs you the ability.*** The chapter leans on it: this is the finding
that gives the chapter its *"why bother."*
