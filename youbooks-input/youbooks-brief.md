# Youbooks — the brief to paste into the idea box

**Do not write a one-line idea.** Its ideation stage ("Agentic Brainstorming") exists to invent ideas using
four frontier models. **A short idea gives it room to invent your book for you.** A complete brief leaves it
nothing to brainstorm — that is the whole countermeasure.

---

## If the box accepts a few hundred words, paste this

    Title: You Are Using AI Wrong — And It Isn't Your Fault, by Michael C. Barnes.

    Do not invent a book. Execute the plan in the attached source document
    (youbooks-sources.docx), which contains the thesis, the tone rules, the 27-chapter
    outline, the 21 Rules, and the verified evidence with its citations. Treat that
    document as the authority and this brief as its index.

    - Follow the 27-chapter structure in the sources exactly. Do not add, remove,
      merge or reorder chapters.
    - Ground every chapter in the supplied evidence. Where you state a fact, give it
      as the sources give it — name, date, publication. Invent no facts, no
      statistics, no studies, no quotations.
    - Do not search the web. Do not fill gaps from the web. Do not use general
      knowledge to supply a fact.
    - Match the trained Human voice. Do not fall into a generic nonfiction register.
    - No brainstorming. The ideas in this book are fixed and already supplied.
    - Length about 45,000 words. Audience: practitioners who hit the wall, builders
      about to start, leaders signing the cheques.
    - Stance: empower the user, name the commercial promise plainly, never attack a
      vendor. No villains — only incentive-shaped design.
    - Do not claim or imply that any AI feels, wants, remembers or experiences
      anything.

## If the box is short (say one or two sentences), paste this instead

    Execute the plan in the attached sources — do not invent a book. Follow the
    27-chapter outline exactly, ground every chapter in the supplied evidence with its
    citations, use no web search and no general knowledge for facts, and write in the
    trained Human voice.

**Then make the sources do the rest.** The outline, the Rules, the tone rules and the evidence are all in
the uploaded file — the brief only has to bind that file as the authority rather than the prompt.

## The four switches that decide whether this works

Their own copy states each of these defaults, and every one of them is the wrong setting for this book:

| Step | Their default | What you must do |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Add Sources | **"Optional — Skip if you prefer to work from your brief alone"** | **Upload `youbooks-sources.docx`. Never skip.** Their copy: *"No sources? Skip this step—we'll draft from your brief."* |
| 1. Web search | **"can pull additional web context to fill gaps"** | **Turn it OFF.** This is Rule 1 — the model's knowledge entering as fact. |
| 2. Human model | **"Optional — Use our editorial default"** | **Upload the 61,577-word corpus.** The editorial default is neutral-voice generation. |
| 3. Scope | *"Define topic, audience, length, and chapter depth"* | Fill it from YOUR plan. Do not let it choose the depth. |

## What to watch in chapter one

    does it follow YOUR 27-chapter outline, or invent its own structure?
    does it cite YOUR evidence, or write generic AI commentary?
    does it sound like the voice corpus, or like a book generator?
    is web research actually off, or did the default sneak back?

**Four yeses, carry on. Any clear no, stop.**

The failure will be visible at chapter one, because "Agentic Brainstorming" operates at the start. If it
produced generic AI commentary — the "in today's fast-paced world, AI is transforming everything" register —
then it brainstormed instead of executing, and twenty more chapters of the same is not worth the credits.
